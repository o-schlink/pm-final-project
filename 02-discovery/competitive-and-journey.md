# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Understand one clear and predictable payment commitment—how much is financed, what the monthly payment is, and how that obligation may change over time.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** The financing journey becomes confusing when the real-world situation does not match the product's assumptions: the payer may not be the service recipient, the final amount may still change, or it may be unclear whether the plan covers the total treatment cost, a single invoice, or only a co-payment. The customer is forced to make a financial decision without a clear picture of what they are actually committing to.
Evidence: UXR-02, UXR-03, UXR-08, UXR-11; ISSUE-301, ISSUE-307, ISSUE-312, ISSUE-324.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Treatment plans, invoices and cost estimates; phone/email or messaging with the school/practice; and simple calculators or notes to compare amounts and estimate monthly affordability.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** . Collect the treatment plan, invoice or cost estimate. 2. Compare it with the installment offer to understand what amount is actually financed. 3. Clarify who must sign/pay if payer and service recipient differ. 4. Contact the partner when amounts, reimbursements or future costs are unclear. 5. Recalculate the expected monthly commitment whenever the underlying cost changes.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The process feels most broken when the customer is ready to make a financial commitment but still cannot tell what the installment plan actually covers or how much the obligation may change later. They must manually reconstruct information that should be clear within one payment journey.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** I don't understand what the installment is actually based on — the full treatment cost, my co-payment or just this invoice?” (UXR-08). “I'd use installments, but only if I can immediately see the total amount, monthly payment, duration and any additional cost.” (UXR-11). The issue data reinforces this: payer and recipient cannot be separated (ISSUE-301), financed amounts can change (ISSUE-307), and treatment cost, invoice and financed amount are not clearly distinguished (ISSUE-312).

4-Stage Future-State Journey Map
#	Stage	User Action -> Benefit	Internal State	Pain Point Addressed
1	Identify Responsible Payer	Confirm payer role -> Agreement reaches the financially responsible person.	Core model separates service recipient, payer, decision maker and communication recipient.	Payer and service recipient are wrongly assumed to be the same person.
2	Clarify Financing Basis	Review payment basis -> See what amount the plan covers.	Configurable rules show whether the plan covers total cost, invoice, co-payment or balance.	Customers do not understand what they are actually financing.
3	Understand Predictable Commitment	Choose predictable terms -> Understand monthly cost, duration and changes.	Standardized calculation logic presents total amount, monthly payment, term and possible variation.	Customers lack confidence when costs or invoices may change over time.
4	Manage Changes Over Time	Track changes -> Keep one clear commitment as costs evolve.	Controlled plan updates handle added lessons, invoices, reimbursements or treatment changes.	Changing real-world costs create confusion, manual work or fragmented agreements.


Competitive Advantages Over The Manual Workaround
1. One clear payer journey instead of informal handoffs
   The financially responsible person receives the right information directly, instead of relying on the learner, patient or partner staff to forward and explain it.
2. Configurable vertical fit without bespoke client builds
   Different cost structures can be handled through reusable rules, rather than repeated custom setup for each client or vertical.
3. Higher trust through transparent payment commitment
   The payer can understand amount, duration, monthly cost and change logic before committing, reducing uncertainty and partner support burden.
