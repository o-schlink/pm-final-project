# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Understand one clear and predictable payment commitment: how much is financed, what the monthly payment is, and how that obligation may change over time.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** The financing journey becomes confusing when the real-world situation does not match the product’s assumptions: the payer may not be the service recipient, the final amount may still change, or it may be unclear whether the plan covers the total treatment cost, a single invoice, or only a co-payment. The customer is forced to make a financial decision without a clear picture of what they are actually committing to.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** The Responsible Payer relies on informal handoffs and manual clarification: the learner/patient forwards links or documents, the partner staff explains financing details, and the payer tries to reconcile treatment plans, invoices, co-payments, reimbursements or changing driving-school costs through emails, phone calls and separate documents.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** We must prevent increasing product fragmentation from limiting the scalability of BFS's installment-plan business by replacing repeated client-specific configurations with a standardized, configurable installment-plan framework that dynamically applies the right rules for each customer, transaction, client, and market.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For BFS clients that need flexible installment solutions across different customer groups, markets, and use cases, we will provide a standardized but configurable installment-plan framework that applies reusable rules while allowing controlled variation where needed. This creates faster onboarding, easier adaptation to market and regulatory requirements, and less dependency on bespoke implementations. It is urgent because the current project-based approach limits BFS’s ability to scale efficiently into new verticals while complexity continues to grow

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Responsible Payers struggle when the real-world financing situation does not match the product assumptions: the payer may not be the service recipient, the final amount may still change, or it is unclear whether the plan covers total treatment cost, an invoice, a co-payment or the remaining balance. Evidence: UXR-02, UXR-03, UXR-08, UXR-11; ISSUE-301, ISSUE-307, ISSUE-312, ISSUE-324.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 46.2K of 316K installment agreements, around 14.6%, have a manual contact attempt after creation. These cases show stronger signs of payment or adjustment needs: 34.35% direct debit return rate vs. 13.00% overall, and 14.99% extension rate vs. 6.76% overall. In orthodontics, 920 of 7.12K agreements, around 12.9%, have post-creation contact, suggesting the issue is broader than KFO and linked to post-setup management needs.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Responsible Payer: a parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers. Goal: understand one clear and predictable payment commitment: amount financed, monthly payment, duration and how the obligation may change over time.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Responsible Payers lack a clear and predictable understanding of what they are committing to when payer roles, financed amount, invoices, reimbursements or later cost changes do not fit the current installment journey.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Reduce post-creation confusion and manual contact needs by enabling a scalable installment-plan core that can handle different payer roles, cost structures and change scenarios across verticals without client-specific workarounds.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Post-creation manual contact rate for installment agreements. Baseline: 14.6% overall and 12.9% in KFO. This is the leading signal that the gap is closing if it decreases for agreements using the improved configurable flow.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Agreement completion / activation rate must not drop, and payment health must not worsen. Watch especially direct debit return rate and extension rate, because lower contact volume would be misleading if customers simply fail, abandon or enter worse payment situations.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Review after a meaningful sample of new agreements has completed the first post-creation/payment cycle. Proceed if post-creation manual contact rate decreases versus the 14.6% baseline without harming activation or payment-health guardrails. Pivot if contact reasons show the main driver is not clarity/configuration but affordability, risk, collections or partner behavior.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on qualitative evidence that Responsible Payers struggle to understand what their installment agreement actually covers, and quantitative evidence that 46.2K of 316K agreements, around 14.6%, have manual contact after creation, I believe that improving clarity and configurability of the installment journey for Responsible Payers will result in fewer post-creation support needs, as measured by a 10% relative reduction in post-creation manual contact rate. I will protect agreement completion / activation rate and payment health, especially direct debit return rate and extension rate, and will make a go/no-go decision after a meaningful sample of new agreements has completed the first post-creation/payment cycle.
